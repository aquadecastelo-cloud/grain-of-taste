02 · to weighmust have · no real swap

Kitchen scale, 1 g steps

Bread is chemistry. A cup of flour can weigh 120 g or 160 g — that's a different dough.

No scale? Sorry, but you'll need to buy one :(

  1. aHonestly — get one. The cheapest digital scale works. It's the one tool with no good swap.
  2. bFor starter and salt: 1 g precision matters most. 7 g of salt vs 10 g is a different bread.

Grams, not cups

03 · to mixmust have · but you already own one

Big bowl + spoon

The dough grows while it rises — by 50–60% in this recipe. Take a bowl with plenty of room to spare.

No bowl or spoon? Use:

  1. aAny pot or a large food container — at least 3 litres for 1 kg of dough.
  2. bYour hand instead of a spoon — wet it first, dough won't stick.
  3. cAn old bank card instead of a dough scraper — to scrape the bowl and shape the dough.

No mixer needed. Ever

04 · to proofeasy swap

Proofing basket (banneton)

Holds the shape overnight in the fridge, so the loaf doesn't spread flat.

Tip: rice flour on the towel — nothing sticks.

No basket? These work just as well:

  1. 1A bowl lined with a kitchen towel — cotton or linen, not terry, and flour it well.
  2. 2A colander + towel — air flows from below, crust gets a nice skin.
  3. 3A plastic container with a lid — no cap needed, the lid keeps it from drying.
05 · to bakeeasy swap

Cast iron pot with a lid

Traps steam for the first 20 minutes — that's what lets the loaf rise and open.

Steam = rise = open crumb

No cast iron? These work just as well:

  1. ✓A roasting bag on a tray — cut it open after 20 min, like taking the lid off. Check the pack: it must be rated for 250 °C.
  2. ✓Any oven-safe pot with a lid — ceramic, glass, steel. Check the handles and the maximum temperature. Glass hates sudden changes: never put it cold into a hot oven or onto a cold wet surface.
  3. ✓A tray + a pan of boiling water below it — steam without a lid. Remove the water after 20 min.
06 · to scoreeasy swap

Razor blade or knife

A cut tells the bread where to open. Without it, it bursts wherever it wants.

No blade? These work just as well:

  1. ✓The sharpest knife you have — serrated works too. One quick confident cut.
  2. ✓Kitchen scissors — snip a few "spikes" on top. Looks great.
  3. ✓A razor on a wooden coffee stirrer — a DIY blade holder that costs nothing.

Cold dough cuts easier

07 · to handlemust have · swap the type, not the job

Parchment + oven mitts

The pot is 250 °C. Parchment lets you lower the dough in without touching it.

No parchment or mitts? Use:

  1. aA teflon baking mat — reusable, lasts for years.
  2. bA thick folded towel instead of mitts — dry only. A wet towel burns through instantly.

Please don't skip the mitts. 250 °C doesn't forgive mistakes :(

08 · to cooleasy swap

Cooling rack

The loaf keeps steaming for an hour. On a flat plate, that steam turns the bottom soggy.

No rack? These work just as well:

  1. ✓The rack from your oven — pull it out, let the loaf cool on top.
  2. ✓Two wooden spoons on the table — lay the loaf across, air flows underneath.
  3. ✓Stand the loaf on its side — lean it on something, turn it once or twice.

And wait 1 hour before cutting — the crumb is still setting.

09 · to checknice to have · very useful for repeatable results

Probe thermometer

In this recipe I keep the dough at 23–25 °C. You can bake your first loaf without it — but a thermometer is what makes your results repeatable.

No thermometer?

  1. aUse water from the fridge in the mix — keeps you safely below 26 °C most of the year.
  2. bWatch the dough, not the clock — +50–60% in volume, bubbles, a jiggle.

You DON'T need: a mixer, a proofer, special flour from abroad.
